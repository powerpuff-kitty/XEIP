# ADR 0006: Bounded durable delivery store with restart-surviving cursors

Status: Proposed (design only; not implemented). Date: 2026-10-09. This decision records a design direction for [issue 4](https://github.com/powerpuff-kitty/XEIP/issues/4) and [issue 21](https://github.com/powerpuff-kitty/XEIP/issues/21). It does not approve production deployment, and nothing described here is implemented, tested or benchmarked.

## Context

[Issue 4](https://github.com/powerpuff-kitty/XEIP/issues/4) requires a delivery state machine, correlation, expiry, deduplication and ordering scopes, stable reconnect cursors, and an offline/persistent delivery profile, while forbidding a false exactly-once claim. [Issue 21](https://github.com/powerpuff-kitty/XEIP/issues/21) requires bounded persistent queues with retention and TTL, resume cursors for dropped connections, duplicate handling under published semantics, and throughput/failure-injection measurements.

The current work is an incomplete slice. [ADR 0002](0002-local-replay-window.md) bounds suppression of identical authenticated retries but stores only digests and never replays them. [ADR 0003](0003-local-delivery-resume.md) adds per-session sequencing and bounded reconnect resume but is explicitly in-memory and restart-loses. [ADR 0005](0005-local-receipts.md) adds advisory per-principal receipts correlated against that in-memory log. [core.md](../core.md) reserves persistent replay and idempotency for v0.2 and states that the base relay has no delivery guarantee. [security.md](../security.md) requires defining retention, expiry, redaction and recovery for stored data, and [threat-model.md](../threat-model.md) T7 records plaintext/token exposure through files as a residual risk. The [delivery profile](../local-delivery.md) states retention is memory-only, lost on restart, and bounded by entry count rather than total bytes.

Restart-surviving cursors and bounded offline recovery therefore require a persisted sequencer, a persisted backlog and an explicit retention policy. That is a storage and recovery decision that must be taken once, before code, because it fixes on-disk format, durability, concurrency and confidentiality properties.

## Decision

Adopt the design in [../local-durable.md](../local-durable.md). In summary:

1. **Add `xeip.local-durable/0.1`**, explicitly enabled with `createRelay({ token | admission, delivery: { ... }, durable: { backend, dir, retentionMs, maxEntriesPerSession, maxSessions, maxBytes, fsync, lock } })`. Durable requires the [delivery](../local-delivery.md) profile because that profile owns the `seq`/`Last-Event-ID`/`after`/`subscribe.after` framing contract; durable changes only the backing store.
2. **Keep the core transport-neutral.** The relay talks to a `DurableStore` abstraction with `reserveSeq`, `append`, `since`, `lookup`, receipt and compaction operations. The recommended default is a dependency-free segmented append-only log with a checksummed record format and an atomically swapped manifest; SQLite (WAL, `(session, seq)` primary key) is the alternative. No envelope field changes.
3. **Persist the per-session sequencer.** Reserve and durably record the monotonic counter before reporting durable acceptance. On restart, `nextSeq = highWater + 1`; reconstruct from the max retained record when the counter is missing; never reuse a `seq`, even after eviction or compaction. A reserved-but-unappended `seq` is a permanent hole that produces a gap, not a rollback.
4. **Bound the store explicitly** by `retentionMs` (wall clock, since monotonic time does not survive restart), `maxEntriesPerSession`, `maxSessions` and a total `maxBytes` budget the in-memory profile lacks. Compact crash-safely and treat interior corruption as fail-closed; discard only a torn tail and downgrade health to `recovered`.
5. **Extend the cursor contract, not the envelope.** A client cursor survives restart within retention for the same store; an in-memory-epoch cursor, a cursor outside retention or a cursor across a hole yields the existing explicit `xeip.gap`/`{type:"gap"}` signal.
6. **State the guarantee honestly: at-least-once, never exactly-once.** Duplicate suppression remains the bounded in-memory [replay](../local-replay.md) ledger; consumers must deduplicate on envelope `id`. Durable receipts persist the `(principal, session, seq)` correlation within the same bounds and remain advisory, per-principal and non-broadcast.
7. **One writer, owner-only, plaintext at rest.** A store directory is single-writer with an exclusive OS lock, `0700`/`0600` permissions, and **no per-record encryption** in 0.1; at-rest confidentiality depends on host and filesystem controls. This is recorded as residual risk, not a control.

## Alternatives

- **Keep the in-memory [delivery](../local-delivery.md) profile only.** Rejected for this design step: it cannot meet issue #4's persistent/offline criterion or issue #21's restart recovery, and it cannot offer a stable cursor across restarts.
- **Filesystem, dependency-free segmented append-only log (recommended).** Adopted as the default: it matches the repository's no-dependency relay, makes the record/manifest format explicit, and makes torn-tail recovery and compaction auditable. Cost: more bespoke crash-safety and corruption code to verify.
- **SQLite backend.** Adopted as the alternative: mature transactionality, WAL durability and simple `(session, seq)` queries. Cost: a pinned dependency (or the experimental `node:sqlite`) and a less transparent recovery story for the repository's minimal-dependency posture. Kept selectable rather than mandated.
- **External broker (Kafka/NATS/Redis Streams/etc.).** Deferred/rejected for 0.1: it introduces a new trust boundary, operational surface and confidentiality/retention model that [security.md](../security.md) has not reviewed, and it conflicts with the current loopback, dependency-free local scope. A broker adapter may be designed later alongside federation ([issue 23](https://github.com/powerpuff-kitty/XEIP/issues/23)).
- **Make replay deduplication durable to obtain exactly-once.** Rejected: persisting accepted-ID digests can suppress a re-acceptance at the relay but cannot prevent duplicate application effects; it would invite an exactly-once overclaim that [core.md](../core.md) forbids.
- **Encrypt each record in 0.1.** Deferred: it requires key management, rotation and recovery that are not designed; relying on OS/full-disk encryption is the honest current boundary.
- **Multi-writer, clustered or replicated store.** Deferred: cross-process sequencing and cross-relay cursors are out of scope until identity, retention and recovery are reviewed together.
- **Silently skip dropped entries on resume.** Rejected, consistent with [ADR 0003](0003-local-delivery-resume.md): loss must surface as a gap.

## Consequences

Positive: a concrete, bounded design now addresses issue #4's persistent/offline and stable-cursor criteria and issue #21's retention/TTL/resume criteria without changing the 0.1 schemas, transports or routing; it composes with the existing replay, delivery and receipt profiles; it defines an on-disk format, a recovery posture and a guarantee statement that can be implemented and tested against.

Negative and limiting: nothing is implemented, so every acceptance criterion that depends on code, conformance tests or benchmarks remains unmet; issue #4 and issue #21 stay open. Durable retention introduces a real plaintext-at-rest exposure (host and filesystem are trusted, no E2EE), a wall-clock dependency with skew/rollback limitations, and bespoke crash-safety/corruption-handling risk. Single-writer locking means no replication or cross-relay cursors. A reserved hole is an accepted, visible loss rather than an atomic guarantee. Receipts gain persistence but remain advisory and cannot prove processing. The design adds no production identity, multi-tenant isolation, encryption or independent security review. See [../local-durable.md](../local-durable.md) for the contract; implementation checkpoints would follow the pattern of [ADR 0003](0003-local-delivery-resume.md), and the issue #21 benchmark/failure-injection scenarios would have to be run and published rather than asserted.
